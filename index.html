Cover letter supplement

Before I wrote my resume,
I ran a listening tour.

This document is a brief account of that process — who I talked to, what they said, and how their feedback shaped how I positioned myself as a candidate.

The method

Before tailoring a single resume bullet, I ran a structured Reverse Exit Interview and Golden Question process — a methodology for gathering candid, direct feedback from former colleagues, collaborators, and clients. This was my listening tour.

Over several weeks, I held 13 conversations with people who had worked alongside me across different roles and contexts — program staff, partner leads, clients, and peers in adjacent industries. Each conversation followed the same framework: what did I do well, what could I have done better, and if you were in my shoes, how would you approach this search?

I took raw notes. Then I coded them. The patterns you see below are not self-assessment — they are what the people I worked with actually said.

What I did with it

The listening tour fed directly into my Mnookin Two-Pager — a structured self-assessment framework that asks you to name what energizes you, what drains you, and what you're actually good at. What my colleagues said became the evidence base for what I wrote down. Their words confirmed some things I suspected, sharpened others, and gave me permission to stop hedging on a few more. The result wasn't just a resume framing exercise — it became the organizing logic for how I tell my story and where I focus my energy.

What I confirmed

I build relationships and systems — and the best roles let me do both at once

My education domain knowledge is a differentiator in tech

One-to-many programs and communities of practice are where I do my best work

What I'm working on

More explicit delegation — naming steps, not just outcomes

Earlier, more direct feedback when something isn't working

Clearer signaling when decisions come from above vs. from me
